When I tell investors GitDealFlow watches GitHub to surface deals early, the first response is almost always the same:

"Isn't that just noise?"

Fair question. Raw GitHub data is noisy. Commit counts alone tell you nothing. A bot can inflate them. A hackathon can spike them. A single developer pushing config files looks the same as a team shipping features.

But here's what changes everything: the tool doesn't look at raw numbers. It looks for the moment a startup starts shipping faster than it normally does.

When a company's engineering output breaks sharply from its own usual pace, that's not noise. Something changed inside that company. They hired. They found product-market fit. They're preparing to launch.

In our analysis across 500 startups, the ones whose contributor count jumped 2x within 14 days had a strong correlation with a fundraise or major product launch within 60 days.

The signal is there. You just need something watching for it — and you don't have to be the most analytical firm to use it. The tool does the watching; you just look at the names.
