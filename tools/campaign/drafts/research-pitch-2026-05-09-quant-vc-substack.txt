SUBJECT: SSRN: GitHub commit velocity leads VC announcements by 7 weeks (n=12k)

Hi,

I run gitdealflow.com — an open-methodology research project on which public GitHub repository signals are leading indicators for institutional VC funding announcements. SSRN-published: abstract=6606558.

Headline finding from the regression: a sustained commit-velocity spike (>40% over a 14-day rolling window vs. the prior 90-day baseline) precedes the public fundraise announcement by a median of 7 weeks. 90% confidence interval: 4-13 weeks. The window is consistent across pre-seed through Series B in a sample of 12,000+ public repos.

Three other signals survive alongside velocity in the multi-variate regression:
— Contributor-diversity Gini coefficient drops from ~0.7 to ~0.45 in the lead-up to institutional rounds
— Stars-to-PR ratio rebound after a public spec-cut
— Dependent-count growth on framework / library repos

Two-of-four firing is the practical threshold; the false-positive rate at that threshold is ~22% in our sample.

I think the result is interesting for your readership specifically because it's reproducible from public GitHub APIs alone (no proprietary telemetry, no API key gating), and because the lead-time window is wide enough to be practically useful for pre-announcement deal sourcing — not just retrospective curiosity.
