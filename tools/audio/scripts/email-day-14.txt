Day fourteen. The Data Nerd. The deal I missed because I trusted the deck.

This one is harder to write, so I'll keep it short.

A friend of mine sent me a deck for a Series A round in twenty-twenty-three. The deck was clean. The numbers were neat. The team slide had the right logos. I passed because the market timing felt off and the moat felt thin. I told myself I had done the work.

I had not done the work. I had read a deck.

The same week I passed, the company shipped three open-source repositories that, in retrospect, were the entire moat. Not the SaaS product the deck was pitching. The open-source layer underneath it that was about to become the default in a sub-category nobody was tracking yet. By the end of the quarter, the company had been the rate-limiting step in three other rounds I tried to get into. By the end of the year, the round I passed was three times oversubscribed at three times the valuation.

If I had read the GitHub instead of the deck, I would have known by the second slide that the deck was the wrong artifact to evaluate the company on.

This is the discipline the dashboard enforces. Read the code, then read the deck. In that order. Always.
