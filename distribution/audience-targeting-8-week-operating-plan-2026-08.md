# GitDealFlow audience and targeting, eight-week operating plan

Created: 2026-08-21
Owner: GitDealFlow growth
Purpose: turn the existing audience research into a repeatable investor-community test. This is an operating document, not public copy.

## Decision: one primary ICP for this test

**Primary ICP:** a seed-stage investor or scout at a small fund, syndicate, or operator-led vehicle, focused on developer tools, open-source infrastructure, AI infrastructure, or technical B2B software, who needs a lower-cost way to discover and shortlist technically active companies.

Why this group:

- GitDealFlow observes public GitHub activity across 350+ startups in 15 sectors.
- The internal Dream 100 says the strongest fit is the investor for whom commit velocity is already a useful sourcing input.
- It removes the weak-fit personas from the next eight weeks: consumer-focused investors, relationship-only pre-seed investors, founders seeking capital, and large funds likely to build internally.

Secondary personas stay visible on site. They do not receive community time during this test.

## Participation rule

The first four contributions in each gated or new community are link-free, product-free, and self-contained. Do not name GitDealFlow unless the discussion directly asks for a relevant public data source or a community lead approves it.

Every contribution log must contain: date, public URL where available, target community, topic, contribution type, reply count after seven days, ownership disclosure status, and next action.

Stop a community test after four useful contributions without a genuine reply. Record that result and replace the channel only after reviewing the evidence.

## Measurement and decision rule

A qualified result is one of: a reply from a relevant investor, a community conversation, an invited public-source share, a tracked visit with a relevant follow-up, or a public placement naming and linking GitDealFlow.

A post published, a view count, or a generic like is not a qualified result.

At week eight, retain a community only if it produced at least one qualified result or shows clear recurring peer interaction. Otherwise replace it with the best evidence-backed alternative.

### Weekly measurement additions

Every active channel row must record:

1. **Qualified visitors**: people who arrive from an external source and then take a relevant next action. Keep raw traffic separate.
2. **Cash cost**: direct spend for that channel in the reporting week.
3. **Labour cost**: minutes worked multiplied by the agreed internal hourly rate. If no rate is set, report minutes beside CPQV instead of inventing a cost.
4. **CPQV**: `(cash cost + labour cost) / qualified visitors`. Report `not yet measurable` when qualified visitors are zero.
5. **Placement depth**: standard, featured, editorial, or partner.
6. **Seven-day afterlife**: qualified visitors and signups after day seven, for every post that is considered for re-sharing.
7. **Earned-to-owned conversion**: tracked visitors, subscribers, and qualified follow-ups from each earned placement.
8. **Reliability**: scheduled runs, successful runs, failed runs, stale runs, and duplicate runs, for the one weekly pipeline.

### First fixes, in order

1. Add a voluntary “How did you first hear about GitDealFlow?” field to new-subscriber capture. Keep an `Other` option and do not make it required.
2. Publish source-tagged public result pages from MCP responses, then measure request to page view to signup. Do not count agent requests as human buyer reach.
3. Create one claim inventory from the locked claims in `AGENTS.md`; sweep public first-party pages and rented profiles against it. `400+`, `4,200`, and any wording that converts 219 observations into financing outcomes are prohibited.
4. Replace overlapping jobs with one weekly distribution pipeline and one weekly report. The report must say what ran, what failed, and which channel produced qualified visitors.
5. Do not start paid spend until the reporting pipeline can run a holdout test and calculate CPQV.
