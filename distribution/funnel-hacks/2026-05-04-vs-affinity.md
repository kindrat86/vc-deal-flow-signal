# Funnel Hack — Affinity Sells CRM, Not Signals (And Why That Matters)

Affinity has the cleanest brand in venture tooling. Their funnel is tight, their demo flow is excellent, their pricing is enterprise-classic ($120K-$200K/year for tier-1 funds). Most of the top-100 funds run on it.

But there's a structural mismatch in their positioning that's worth deconstructing — because it reveals where the next category gets built.

## The hook

Affinity's hook is "the relationship intelligence platform for venture capital." The CTA is "see your network." The product is, fundamentally, a CRM built around your team's email and calendar history, with deal-pipeline tooling layered on top.

What they get right:

1. **Insanely-clean visual brand.** The product looks $200K/year. That's worth real money in the demo cycle.
2. **Single sharp positioning.** "Your network is the deal flow." They never wander into "and also signals" territory. Discipline.
3. **Network-effects defensibility.** Once your fund's email history is in Affinity, switching cost is high. Sticky.

## Where it breaks

The breakdown is at the top of the funnel. Affinity tells you what your **existing** network looks like. It doesn't tell you who you **don't** know yet that's about to break out.

For a tier-1 fund with a 10-year-old network, that's fine. The network is the moat. For a fund with a 2-year-old network — emerging managers, scout-led syndicates, family offices — the network alone is a half-built moat.

Three structural issues with using Affinity alone for sourcing:

1. **Backward-looking by design.** Email + calendar + deal-pipeline data only reflects relationships you already have. By definition it can't surface companies you've never heard of.
2. **Pricing wall.** $120K-$200K/year is fine for tier-1. Disqualifies the entire emerging-manager segment that needs the most help with sourcing.
3. **No external-data layer.** Affinity doesn't ingest GitHub commit graphs, Stripe filings, Hacker News surges, or any other public-data feed. The signal of "the world is changing" doesn't reach the CRM.

## What you can steal from this funnel-hack

Three lessons:

1. **CRM is the table stakes, not the moat.** Every fund will have a CRM by 2030. The next-decade moat is the data layer that surfaces companies your CRM doesn't know yet.
2. **Backward-looking data ≠ leading indicator.** Email and calendar history is the most valuable retroactive dataset in venture. It's also the wrong source for "who's about to break out."
3. **The category gap is "leading-indicator data, plugged into your CRM."** Whoever builds the public-data signal layer that integrates cleanly with Affinity (or replaces it for the lower segment) owns the next decade.
