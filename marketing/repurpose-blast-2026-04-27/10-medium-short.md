Every week, I track engineering signals across 4,200+ startup GitHub orgs. Most weeks produce incremental data. This week produced one of the cleaner Growth-stage signals I have seen this quarter.

**The number:** Akto, the open-source API security testing platform and Y Combinator W22 alum, registered a +75% commit velocity change. 267 commits over 14 days. 53 contributors active.

That number is not a measurement error. It is a genuine framework migration — the signal type that, in our historical dataset, often precedes a new product surface, an enterprise tier, or a fundraising round at Growth stage.

**The backstory**

Akto entered the API security market at the right time. Salt Security, the category leader, raised more than $300M and has been working through layoffs and exec turnover. Noname Security, the most-funded competitor, was acquired by Akamai in 2024 for around $500M. Wallarm has stayed enterprise-only.

That left an opening: the developer-first, open-source alternative with self-host options and a clean API surface. Akto has been quietly building into that slot since 2022 — REST, GraphQL, gRPC out of the box, real GitHub traction, regular releases.

**What framework migration looks like**

In our signal taxonomy, "framework migration" is not just "lots of commits." It is a qualitative classification based on commit patterns: core modules being restructured rather than extended, build pipelines and runtime targets being changed, dependency graphs being upgraded deeply.

These are not bug fixes. They are the foundations being rebuilt — usually because something significant is going to ship on top of them.

At Growth stage, this pattern is meaningful. Teams here do not refactor the core casually. It costs engineering time, focus, and usually carries a clear reason — a new product surface that the old core could not support, an enterprise tier with different runtime requirements, or a fundraising demo where the bar is higher than the prior round.

**The timing**

GitDealFlow flagged this on April 27, 2026. Based on the historical pattern in our dataset, the announcement that explains this engineering surge will likely surface in Crunchbase, TechCrunch, or Akto's own blog within 3 to 6 weeks.

That window — between the GitHub signal and the public announcement — is where early-stage investors find the deals that matter.
