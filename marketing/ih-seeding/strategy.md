# IndieHackers Seeding Strategy: The Data Nerd

## Overview

Build credible presence on IndieHackers over 10+ days before dropping a launch post. Same Brunson "Dig Your Well" principle as Reddit, but adapted for IH's builder-friendly culture.

**Account:** The Data Nerd (just created)
**Profile bio:** "Engineer who noticed GitHub data predicts startup traction months before funding rounds. Now building tools to surface those signals."
**Persona:** The Data Nerd: someone who builds with data, shares insights freely, celebrates other builders. NOT a founder pitching a product.

## IH vs Reddit: Key Differences

| Aspect | Reddit | IndieHackers |
|---|---|---|
| Culture | Skeptical, anti-self-promo | Builder-friendly, supportive |
| Self-promo tolerance | Very low (Phase 1-2 danger zone) | Higher (but earn it first) |
| Best content | Comments on others' posts | Comments + own milestone posts |
| Discovery | Subreddit-based | Feed + topic-based |
| Tone | Casual, slightly edgy | Enthusiastic, constructive |
| Engagement style | Contrarian insights win | Helpful specifics win |

## Three-Phase Timeline

### Phase 1: Pure Value (Days 1-7, April 15-21)
- 3-5 comments per day on trending posts
- ZERO product mentions
- Focus on: congratulating milestones, sharing data insights, asking smart questions
- Reply to every engagement on your comments
- Upvote generously (IH is reciprocal)
- Goal: become a visible, helpful commenter

### Phase 2: Soft Authority (Days 8-11, April 22-25)
- Continue commenting (2-4/day)
- Post ONE "building in public" update: "Tracking engineering signals across 2,000 startups - here's what I'm learning"
- If asked about the tool, share casually
- Start engaging with frequent posters and IH "regulars"
- Goal: get 2-3 people to follow your profile or ask for updates

### Phase 3: Launch Post (Days 12+, April 26+)
- Drop the launch post (adapt from existing marketing/launch-posts/indiehackers-launch.md)
- Should feel like a natural extension of your comment history
- Cross-reference insights you shared in comments
- Goal: the comment history validates you as a real builder, not a drive-by promo

## Comment Style Guide (IH-adapted Data Nerd voice)

1. **Open with genuine appreciation** of what they built/shared. IH is supportive culture.
2. **Bridge to a specific insight** with "One thing I've noticed from tracking engineering data..." or "Interesting — the pattern I see is..."
3. **Be concrete.** Numbers, specific observations, real examples. IH hates generic advice.
4. **Keep it constructive.** Unlike Reddit, don't be contrarian for its own sake. Add to the conversation.
5. **Ask a follow-up question** that shows you actually read their post.
6. **Never use** corporate speak, buzzwords, or "great post!" without substance.
7. **Length:** 80-200 words ideal. IH comments are shorter and punchier than Reddit.

### Good IH comment openers:
- "This is great — congrats on hitting [milestone]."
- "Love seeing the build process. One thing that jumped out..."
- "Interesting approach. I've been tracking something related..."
- "The [specific thing] resonates. From the data side..."

### Bad IH comment openers (avoid):
- "Actually..." (condescending)
- "Have you considered..." (unsolicited advice without context)
- "Check out my tool..." (too early)
- Generic praise without substance

## What to Do When Someone Asks About Your Project

Phase 1 response: "I'm building something in the alternative data space for startup tracking. Still early — mostly just crunching GitHub API data and seeing what patterns emerge. Will share more when it's ready!"

Phase 2 response: "Yeah! I'm building GitDealFlow — it tracks engineering signals (commit velocity, contributor growth) across startup GitHub orgs to spot breakout companies early. Existing tools like Harmonic/Dealroom charge $10K+/year and require demo calls — I wanted something transparent and self-serve. Still in beta but happy to share if you're curious."

Phase 3 response: Full launch post with link.

## Competitive Positioning (weave into conversations when relevant)

Use these angles when someone discusses deal flow tools, expensive platforms, or data access in VC:

- **Price**: "Deal flow platforms like Harmonic and Dealroom charge $10K+/year. I wanted something affordable for solo angels, not just funds."
- **Access**: "Every platform I looked at requires a demo call to even see the product. That felt backwards for 2026."
- **Transparency**: "They use proprietary black-box data. I use public GitHub data — you can verify every number yourself."
- **Signal type**: "Those platforms tell you about rounds after they're announced. Engineering signals show you the acceleration before the pitch deck exists."

If someone asks "how is this different from Harmonic/Dealroom?": "They charge $10K+/year, gate behind demos, and use proprietary data. I'm self-serve at EUR 9.97/mo with fully transparent public GitHub data. And none of them track engineering momentum — they report lagging indicators."

If someone mentions Forager: "Forager has a free trial but only searches people, not startups or engineering signals."

## Daily Time Budget

- 10 min: scan IH trending posts and recent activity
- 20 min: write and post 3-5 comments
- 5 min: reply to engagement on previous comments
- **Total: 35 min/day**

## Coordination with Reddit Seeding

Both campaigns run in parallel. They share the same persona and insights but the tone differs:
- Reddit: more analytical, slightly edgy, data-first
- IH: more enthusiastic, builder-supportive, insight-sharing

Do NOT cross-post identical comments. Repurpose the insight, rewrite the delivery.
