Eric,

Quick share. We just open-sourced a longitudinal panel of GitHub engineering velocity across 4,200 venture-backed startups. The headline finding: across 219 confirmed fundraise rounds in the panel, the engineering acceleration preceded the announcement by 21 to 47 days, with a median of 47.

Three pieces of it that might fit a future Newcomer issue:

1. The dataset is fully open under CC BY 4.0 on Zenodo (zenodo.org/records/19650920) with the methodology paper on SSRN (ssrn.com/abstract=6606558). Replicable end-to-end, which we have not seen in the alt-data-for-VC space before.

2. The lead-time distribution by sector is not flat. Infrastructure and dev-tools companies show the cleanest signal (median 51 days lead). AI/ML lags slightly (median 44 days, with more noise from research repos). Consumer tech companies are weakest as expected (mostly invisible because their work is private until launch).

3. The data shows where most generic "GitHub stars" signals fail: the bimodality between pre-MVP teams (private-repo dark) and post-MVP teams (sharp public acceleration). That divide is the reason 18 to 24 months tends to be the earliest interpretable window.

We just published the first weekly Top 100 to Substack (gitdealflow.substack.com/p/top-100-github-signal-startups-week) and the second drops this Sunday. Happy to run the per-sector raw breakouts for anything you might be writing about, ahead of the public digest.

No expectation of a mention. Just a dataset that probably takes other people more interesting directions than us.
