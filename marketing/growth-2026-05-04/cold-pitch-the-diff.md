**Subject**: Quantifying the GitHub-velocity-leads-fundraise hypothesis (47 days, 219 rounds)

Byrne,

Long-time reader. The Diff has touched the "code-side momentum as alt-data" theme a few times and we just put a quantified version of it into the open.

Panel: 4,200 venture-backed startups, weekly GitHub engineering data (commit velocity, contributor delta, repository expansion), matched against 219 confirmed fundraise rounds in 2024 and 2025. The engineering acceleration preceded the announcement by 21 to 47 days, with a median of 47.

Three things in the methodology you would probably find interesting:

1. The contributor-delta component carries the most weight. 78 percent of the 219 rounds showed a +30 percent jump in distinct human authors in the 60 days before the announcement. Hiring is the cleanest leading signal venture has ever had access to in a structured form, because new engineers push their first public commit within 2 to 3 weeks of starting.

2. The signal is bimodal. Pre-MVP teams (most companies before month 18) are systematically dark because the work is in private repos. Post-MVP teams produce a sharp, sector-normalized acceleration that decays roughly two months after the round closes. The implication is that engineering signals are not a startup-discovery tool from day zero, they are a fundraise-timing tool from month 18 onward.

3. We sector-normalized on 20 GitHub-topic clusters and the 47-day median holds in 19 of the 20. The exception is research-heavy AI/ML where the signal is noisier because of academic-style commits.
