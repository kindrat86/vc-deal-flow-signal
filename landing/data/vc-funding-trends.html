2026 Funding Landscape

Global VC funding is tracking toward $350-400B in 2026, driven by AI/ML, climate tech, and defense tech.

GitDealFlow's leading signals suggest acceleration in H2 2026, particularly in AI infrastructure and climate technology.

Seed-stage valuations remain elevated ($8-12M median pre-money). Series A valuations have normalized from 2021 peaks.

Where the Capital Is Going

The 2026 market is concentrated rather than broad. AI/ML captures the largest share of funding and the most attention, with infrastructure, applied layers, and tooling all competing for capital. Climate tech follows as policy tailwinds and energy demand push capital toward grids, storage, and industrial efficiency. Fintech has stabilized after the 2022-2024 reset, while healthtech and defense tech round out the top tiers. What is notable is not just where the money goes, but where it does not: general-purpose SaaS and consumer apps are seeing a smaller share as investors concentrate into themes with visible secular tailwinds.

Stage Dynamics

Early-stage remains the most active part of the market. Seed rounds are plentiful and priced stably, because the denominator problem that froze late-stage funds never hit seed investors as hard, small checks, high optionality, and faster feedback. Series A is the bottleneck: the bar has risen to clear evidence of product-market fit, and the companies that clear it do so with momentum that is observable before the announcement. Late-stage is the most selective, with longer diligence cycles and more insistence on path-to-profitability.

What the Leading Signals Show

Funding announcements are lagging indicators, they describe a market that already moved. The leading indicators are the teams themselves: hiring, shipping, and expanding before the round. GitDealFlow tracks commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion across 350+ startups, with 219 startup-period observations across 55 startups in its research panel and a typical 21-47 day lead between engineering acceleration and the public announcement. When engineering output accelerates across a sector, funding in that sector usually follows, which is why the signal data points to continued acceleration in AI infrastructure and climate technology into H2 2026.

How to Use This Analysis

For investors, the practical use is allocation: overweight sectors where engineering acceleration is broad, and treat funding trend lines as confirmation, not prediction. For founders, the lesson is timing: raise when your own momentum is peaking, not when the macro headlines look best. Frequently Asked Questions

What sectors are getting the most VC funding in 2026?

AI/ML (~30%), climate tech (~15%), fintech (~12%), healthtech (~10%), and defense tech (~10%). GitDealFlow tracks engineering momentum in all 15 sectors.

Is the Series A crunch real?

Yes, the bar for Series A has risen: investors want demonstrated product-market fit and durable retention, not just growth. Companies with verifiable momentum clear it faster.

How can I track funding trends in real time?

Follow the leading indicators: engineering momentum per sector via signals.gitdealflow.com, then confirm with funding announcements as they land.

What is a funding 'crunch' vs a correction?

A correction is prices adjusting; a crunch is volume drying up. 2026 looks like a correction in most sectors, deals still close, but at disciplined prices.

Which signal moves first, panel or funding data?

The panel, by construction: its breakout flags precede announcements by a measured 21 to 47 days across the 350+ organization panel. Funding databases register the same event at announcement time.

Can engineering data predict totals like the $350-400B figure?

No. It ranks teams and sectors by acceleration; macro totals depend on allocation decisions the panel does not see. Use it for direction and timing, not aggregate size.
