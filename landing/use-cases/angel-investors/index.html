GitDealFlow for Angel Investors

How angel investors use early signals to find pre-seed opportunities.

Angel investors operate at the earliest stage, where data is scarcest. There is no revenue to inspect, often no press coverage, and no database entry until long after the interesting moment. GitDealFlow levels the playing field by giving angels the same signal access that large funds enjoy, without the enterprise contracts or the analyst teams.

Why Angels Need Signal-Based Sourcing

Angels cannot compete with funds on network or deal flow volume. But you can compete on timing. If you spot a promising startup before it hits the major databases, you can reach out directly and negotiate better terms, and you can get into rounds that are not yet being shopped around. GitDealFlow reads the public GitHub activity of 350+ startups and tracks commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion. When a small team's engineering starts accelerating, a pre-seed or seed round typically follows within 21-47 days, a pattern documented in a research panel of 219 fundraises.

How GitDealFlow Works for You

Every Sunday you receive five names by email, selected for accelerating engineering momentum. Each name can be verified on GitHub in a few minutes, look at the repositories, the commit cadence, the growing contributor list, before you spend any time on outreach. The digest is free, unsubscribes in one click, and is designed for exactly the workflow an angel has: a short weekly review, not a full-time research desk.

  • Find pre-seed companies months before they appear in any database or press release.
  • Verify momentum yourself on GitHub, the same public data the signals are computed from.
  • Reach founders while the round is still being assembled, when access and terms are best.
  • Track candidates over weeks to confirm the acceleration is real and sustained.

A Simple Weekly Workflow

Monday: skim the five names, pick the one or two that fit your thesis. Tuesday: spend 20 minutes on GitHub verifying their momentum. Wednesday: send one personalized note referencing something specific from their public engineering work. That is the whole system, repeat weekly, log what you saw, and let the pipeline build. The methodology behind the digest is published on SSRN under a CC BY 4.0 license with an open dataset, so you always know exactly how the signal was computed.
