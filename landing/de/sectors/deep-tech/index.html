Deep Tech Startups — Funding Signals & Deal Flow

Frontier research, quantum, and advanced-computing software — tracked at the GitHub-org level across ~400 startups.

GitDealFlow tracks frontier research, quantum, and advanced-computing software startup momentum derived from public GitHub activity: commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion. We surface breakout deep tech engineering teams 3–6 weeks before their fundraise is announced — early enough to matter, late enough to be real. The deep tech sector bucket includes quantum computing stacks; advanced materials modeling; satellite and space infrastructure; photonics and optics simulation; scientific computing frameworks.

Deep Tech sector overview

Deep-tech momentum signals come from research-stack commits, simulation-framework work, and infrastructure repo growth. GitDealFlow weights simulation and infra repos highest — pure research output rarely signals commercial traction.

The sector covers approximately 5 active sub-focus areas. Teams that ship weekly commits across multiple sub-areas are the strongest predictor of near-term commercial traction; single-repo teams are typically earlier-stage and noisier.

Recent deep tech funding trends

Quantum infrastructure and space-software have led deep-tech GitHub momentum in 2026. Teams shipping simulation and infra improvements weekly are typically 6–10 weeks from a milestone-driven raise.

The pattern repeats across sectors: engineering acceleration in production-deployment repos (serving, integration, SDK) precedes fundraise announcements by 3–6 weeks. Research-output acceleration alone is a weaker signal — it correlates with academic output, not commercial traction.

Top deep tech signals to track

The GitDealFlow methodology weights the following signals most heavily when scoring deep tech startup momentum:

  • Simulation-framework commit velocity
  • Infrastructure repo contributor growth
  • Research-stack releases
  • Hardware-integration creation
