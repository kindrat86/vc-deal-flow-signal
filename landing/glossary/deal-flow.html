Deal Flow

Definition

Deal flow is the stream of startup investment opportunities an investor sees and evaluates over time. The term covers both sides of the funnel: how many opportunities arrive, and how many of them are worth acting on. Two investors can see the same number of companies and have completely different deal flow, because what matters is access to companies that fit their thesis at a stage and price where they can still act.

Where deal flow comes from

Most investors assemble deal flow from several sources at once:

  • Referrals and warm introductions. Founders, other investors, and operators vetting opportunities before they arrive. Historically the highest-quality source, but limited by the size of your network.
  • Accelerators and demo days. A concentrated batch of early companies, though the best batches are competitive to access.
  • Inbound pitches. Cold decks and founder emails. High volume, variable quality, heavy screening cost.
  • Scout programs. Operators and founders source deals on behalf of a fund in exchange for carry.
  • Data tools. Databases like Crunchbase or PitchBook list companies after announcements; signal tools like GitDealFlow surface companies before rounds become public.

Why timing decides deal flow quality

A deal seen early and a deal seen late are different investments. Once a fundraise is announced, the round is often partially allocated, the valuation has moved, and competition compresses the time you have to decide. That is why serious investors pay for access and speed, not just volume: the same company, seen three weeks earlier, can be the difference between getting an allocation and watching from the sidelines.

This is also why leading indicators matter. Sustained engineering acceleration has historically preceded fundraise announcements by three to six weeks, which turns deal flow from a referral lottery into a weekly, ranked list of companies quietly heating up.

Key points

  • Deal flow = the stream of investment opportunities an investor sees, judged on quality and timing, not just volume.
  • Sources stack: referrals, accelerators, inbound, scouts, and data tools each cover a different slice.
  • The same deal changes character once a round is public: allocation, price, and competition all move against you.
  • Leading signals like engineering acceleration surface companies before the announcement cycle does.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is deal flow in venture capital?

Deal flow is the stream of startup investment opportunities an investor receives and evaluates over time. It is judged on both volume (how many deals arrive) and quality (how many are worth investing in). Strong deal flow means consistent access to companies that fit the investor's thesis.

How do investors get deal flow?

Common sources: founder referrals and warm introductions, accelerator demo days, other investors sharing rounds, inbound pitch decks, scout programs, and data tools that surface companies early. Most established investors combine several of these.

What does good deal flow look like?

Good deal flow is thesis-relevant, arrives early, and converts. A stream of 100 random decks is worse than 15 companies that fit your stage and sector and reach you before the round is competitive. Quality and timing beat raw volume.

How early should you see a startup before its round?

Ideally weeks before the round is public. Once a fundraise is announced the round is often allocated, and valuation rises. Leading indicators, such as sustained engineering acceleration on public GitHub, have historically appeared 3-6 weeks before fundraise announcements.
