GitDealFlow for Impact Investors

Why Impact Investors Use GitDealFlow

Impact investing has a sourcing problem: the most mission-aligned companies are often the hardest to evaluate. Early-stage climate tech, edtech, and healthtech startups rarely have meaningful revenue, and their social or environmental impact is difficult to quantify from a pitch deck alone. Impact investors need evidence about what the team is actually building, and engineering activity is one of the few public, continuous sources of that evidence.

GitDealFlow builds proprietary deal flow with engineering momentum data. It reads the public GitHub activity of 350+ startups and tracks commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion. When a team's engineering accelerates, a fundraise typically follows 21-47 days later, a pattern documented in a research panel of 219 fundraises. For impact investors, that window is when the best deals are accessible and the terms are most favorable.

Impact sectors produce some of the strongest engineering signals. Software is the backbone of most climate, health, and education ventures, from energy modeling platforms to telehealth infrastructure to adaptive learning tools, and much of that work happens in public on GitHub. GitDealFlow's sector-level tracking helps impact investors find startups that are both mission-aligned and commercially serious.

How GitDealFlow Works for You

Every Sunday you receive five names by email, selected for accelerating engineering momentum. Each signal is computed from public GitHub data, so you can verify it yourself before any outreach, open the repositories, look at the commit history, and judge the team's trajectory with your own eyes.

  • Screen climate, health, and education startups for real engineering momentum, not just mission language.
  • Prioritize which founders to meet first based on verifiable building activity.
  • Track momentum in your impact sectors week over week without manual scraping.
  • Benchmark a startup against others in its sector before writing a check.
