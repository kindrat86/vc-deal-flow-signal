Why Code-Side Sourcing Is a Leading Signal While Funding Databases Are Lagging

The core asymmetry: engineering work happens before the deck is written, before the intro circulates, before the press release. A startup that just closed a seed round hires engineers who push code to public repositories within days. That code velocity surge shows up on GitHub immediately. The Series A announcement shows up on Crunchbase 21-47 days later, after the round has closed and the allocation is gone.

The SSRN research panel (n=219 confirmed fundraises) found that the composite signal of commit velocity acceleration combined with low top-contributor concentration (Gini coefficient under 0.30) made a startup 3.4x more likely to announce a Series A within 60 days than velocity acceleration alone. This finding is published, auditable, and reproducible from the open dataset.

Crunchbase records the outcome. GitDealFlow reads the input. Both are useful. Only one gives you a head start.

Key Differences

Data model: Crunchbase is a structured company graph: funding rounds, investors, acquisitions, headcount, revenue estimates. It is built for research workflows where completeness matters more than recency. GitDealFlow is a weekly-ranked signal feed: commit velocity change, contributor spikes, repo expansion. It is built for sourcing workflows where recency IS the product.

Update cadence: Crunchbase updates when funding events are announced or when company profiles are edited. GitDealFlow refreshes every Monday morning from the prior week's GitHub activity, so the leaderboard always reflects the most recent engineering momentum.

Verifiability: Crunchbase data accuracy depends on proprietary collection and curation. GitDealFlow's methodology is published open-access on SSRN (abstract=6606558) with a CC BY 4.0 dataset. Any investor can reproduce the regression and verify the lead-time claim independently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GitDealFlow a replacement for Crunchbase?

Crunchbase records funding rounds, investors, and valuations after they become public. What data does GitDealFlow have that Crunchbase doesn't?

GitDealFlow tracks public GitHub engineering signals: 14-day commit velocity change, contributor diversity (Gini coefficient), repository expansion rate, deploy frequency spikes, and infrastructure buildout. These are leading indicators of startup momentum not available in any funding database. The 3.4x Series A lift finding (velocity × contributor diversity composite) is unique to GitDealFlow's methodology.

How was the 21-47 day lead time measured?
