GitDealFlow vs PitchBook: Pre-Round vs Post-Round Signals

PitchBook is the industry standard for private market data — funding rounds, valuations, cap tables. GitDealFlow is a lead indicator: tracks the engineering acceleration that happens 21–47 days BEFORE a startup announces its round.

The fundamental difference: timing

PitchBook is a comprehensive database of the private markets: funding rounds, valuations, cap tables, fund performance, and company profiles, maintained by analysts and sourced from filings and announcements. When a round closes, it appears in PitchBook — thoroughly documented, historically rich, and searchable across every dimension an LP or GP could want. Its purpose is understanding what has happened in the market, and it does that better than any other tool.

GitDealFlow is not a database of records — it is a feed of signals. It reads public GitHub activity across 350+ startups and tracks commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion. When a team starts shipping at an accelerating pace, GitDealFlow flags them — typically 21-47 days before the funding round appears anywhere else, including PitchBook. The method is published on SSRN under CC BY 4.0 with an open dataset, so the signal is auditable rather than black-box.

GitDealFlow vs PitchBook

  • Signal timing: GitDealFlow = before the round. PitchBook = after close.
  • Data source: GitDealFlow = public GitHub activity. PitchBook = funding announcements and filings.
  • Price: GitDealFlow = free email signal, paid tiers for Dashboard and Insider. PitchBook = enterprise licensing.
  • Output: GitDealFlow = a short weekly list of accelerating startups. PitchBook = deep records on every company.

When to use which

  • Use PitchBook when: you need a complete, historical view of a company or market — prior rounds, investors, valuations, and fund data. It is the diligence and reporting layer.
  • Use GitDealFlow when: you want to find companies before the market knows them — new pipeline, early conversations, and pre-emptive diligence at pre-announcement prices.
  • Use both together: GitDealFlow supplies the names while they are still cheap; PitchBook supplies the history that turns a name into an investment memo.

The two tools answer different questions. PitchBook answers "what is this company's full story?" GitDealFlow answers "which teams are accelerating right now, and which of them will be raising soon?" For investors whose edge is being early, the second question is where the returns are made.
