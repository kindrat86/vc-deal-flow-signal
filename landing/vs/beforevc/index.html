Choose beforeVC if broad momentum matters most

Choose beforeVC if cross-platform attention, launch momentum, and broad emerging-product discovery matter more than a fundraise-specific signal. It is the better match when Hacker News discussion, Reddit interest, Product Hunt launches, and product buzz are part of your investment thesis.

Choose GitDealFlow if you source technical startups

Choose GitDealFlow if you want a weekly technical-startup sourcing read built around public commit velocity, contributor growth, and infrastructure buildout, with the method and historic panel open to inspection. The question it answers is narrower: which teams are visibly building hard enough that they deserve a founder conversation before a round is public?

The GitDealFlow proof line: Public GitHub signals from 350+ startup orgs across 15 sectors, refreshed weekly. In a documented panel of 219 startup-period observations across 55 startups, the signal appeared 21 to 47 days before the public fundraise announcement. It is a sourcing signal, not investment advice.

The practical workflow

These products can be complementary. Use beforeVC when you want a wider view of early product momentum. Use GitDealFlow when the product itself is public and engineering activity is the signal you want to examine. Once a company makes your short list, use a database such as Crunchbase, Dealroom, or PitchBook to check funding history, investors, and market context.

Three questions to ask before choosing

First, does your thesis depend on technical execution that can be observed in public repositories? Second, do you want a narrow weekly short list or a wider stream of product and community momentum? Third, can you verify the evidence behind each alert without trusting a hidden score? GitDealFlow is the stronger fit when the first and third questions matter most. beforeVC is the stronger fit when breadth across launch and discussion channels matters more. If both kinds of evidence are useful, keep the workflows separate so a burst of public attention is not mistaken for a sustained change in engineering output.
