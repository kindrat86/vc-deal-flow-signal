GitDealFlow vs Crunchbase

Crunchbase is the world's largest startup database, funding rounds, acquisitions, founder profiles, and news. It's the rearview mirror: you see what happened. GitDealFlow is the windshield: you see which startups are accelerating on GitHub right now, 21-47 days before they appear in Crunchbase's funding feed.

What each tool does best

Crunchbase is the reference database of the startup world. If you need to know who founded a company, who invested in it, how much it raised across rounds, or who its competitors are, Crunchbase is the most complete public source. Its strength is breadth and history: it is the place to research a company's past and map an entire market.

GitDealFlow is a signal feed, not a database. It reads public GitHub activity, commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion, across 350+ startups and surfaces the teams that are accelerating. The output is a short list of names delivered weekly by email (five every Sunday), each typically 21-47 days ahead of the funding announcement. Its strength is timing and focus: a tiny list of companies you have never heard of, weeks before the market has.

How they complement each other

The classic workflow uses both. GitDealFlow surfaces the names early; Crunchbase provides the context, who the founders are, what they raised before, who is already in the cap table, and what the comparable companies look like. The signal tells you where to look; the database tells you what you are looking at.

When to use which

  • Use Crunchbase when: researching a known company, building a market map, checking an investor's track record, or finding comparable rounds for valuation work.
  • Use GitDealFlow when: building a fresh pipeline, getting ahead of the market on undiscovered startups, monitoring portfolio-adjacent sectors, or looking for engineering momentum as a diligence signal.
  • Use both when: you get a new name from GitDealFlow and need to diligence it, history from Crunchbase, momentum from GitDealFlow.

Crunchbase reports on what happened. GitDealFlow reports on what is happening inside the companies that will make tomorrow's news. One tells you the price of yesterday's rounds; the other tells you which rounds are being prepared right now.
