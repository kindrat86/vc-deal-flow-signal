GitDealFlow vs PitchBook: At a Glance

PitchBook PitchBook is the gold standard for PE/VC financial data with 3,000+ research analysts, the best post-announcement database but incapable of predicting rounds before they happen. GitDealFlow takes a fundamentally different approach: instead of aggregating funding announcements, we track the engineering activity that precedes them. This means GitDealFlow users see breakout startups 3-6 weeks before any database update.

Key Differences

Data source: PitchBook relies on company filings, press releases, and self-reported data. GitDealFlow reads public GitHub commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion, objective engineering signals no startup can fabricate at scale.

Timeliness: PitchBook updates when funding rounds are announced. GitDealFlow updates weekly based on the prior week's GitHub activity, catching momentum as it builds, not after it's public.

Use case: PitchBook is best for post-announcement research and market mapping. GitDealFlow is built for pre-announcement sourcing, finding deals before they're deals.

Which Tool Should You Use?

Most serious investors use both. PitchBook for due diligence, market context, and confirmed funding data. GitDealFlow for discovering new opportunities, tracking portfolio company momentum, and getting into rounds before they're competitive. They're complementary, not competitive.
