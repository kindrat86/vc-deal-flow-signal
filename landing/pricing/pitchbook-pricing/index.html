PitchBook Pricing: How Much It Costs and What You Get

PitchBook does not publish a price list; every license is a custom quote. Buyers report single-seat licenses around $12,000 to $20,000 per year, 3-user team plans around $25,000 per year, and enterprise deployments from $45,000 up to $70,000 or more per year. There is no permanent free tier, only a trial.

Why PitchBook Has No Public Pricing

PitchBook is sold through a direct enterprise sales motion, not a self-serve checkout. The final number depends on the number of named seats, which data modules and coverage are included, and contract length, so there is no single list price to point at. That is also why the figures above are ranges reported by buyers rather than official numbers: the only way to get a real quote is a sales call.

What Drives the Price

Three levers move a PitchBook quote. Seats are the biggest one: additional seats are commonly quoted at roughly $7,000 per year on top of a base team plan. Data modules matter second: private-market coverage, fund performance, and league tables each add cost. Contract length and renewal terms matter third, since annual and multi-year commitments price lower than quarterly. If you come with a budget constraint stated up front, buyers report meaningful room to negotiate.

What You Get for the Price

For the money you get deep post-announcement financial data: funding rounds, valuations, fund performance, and deal history across private markets, plus the research team's coverage. It is the standard for diligence on companies that are already public about raising. What it does not give you is early signal on companies before they raise, because that data does not exist in a database until the round is announced.
