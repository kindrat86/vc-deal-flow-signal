A beforeVC alternative for investors who source technical startups

GitDealFlow is not a broad startup-buzz tracker. It reads public GitHub engineering acceleration to help angels, scouts, and seed investors find technical teams worth investigating before the funding round is public.

Choose GitDealFlow when your question is:
Which technical startups are visibly building fast enough that I should meet the founder before the round is public?

How GitDealFlow differs from beforeVC

beforeVC is useful for broad startup momentum across GitHub, Hacker News, Reddit, and Product Hunt. GitDealFlow takes a narrower approach. It watches public commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion, then explains the engineering pattern in plain English.

That makes GitDealFlow a better fit when you invest in AI, infrastructure, developer tools, enterprise SaaS, or other technical companies where public product work is a useful sourcing clue. It is not the better choice for every company: stealth teams, closed-source products, and non-technical businesses may not show enough public GitHub activity to evaluate.

Use GitDealFlow to discover, then verify

GitDealFlow is the early-warning layer. Use it to find technical companies early. Use Crunchbase, Dealroom, or PitchBook to verify funding history, investors, and market context once a company is on your list. The tools have different jobs, and using them in that order is more useful than treating any one of them as a complete sourcing stack.

What is documented: GitDealFlow refreshes public GitHub signals weekly across 350+ startup orgs in 15 sectors. The research release contains 219 startup-period observations across 55 startups and no linked funding-event labels. It is a research and diligence input, not investment advice.

Pick beforeVC if this is your priority

Pick beforeVC if you want broader public attention and breakout-product discovery, and you care more about launch, social, community, and cross-platform momentum than a technical engineering signal tied to a specific sourcing workflow.

Run a public engineering check before you choose

The fastest way to understand the difference is to use a real company rather than compare feature lists. Open the free GitHub Momentum Checker, enter an organisation or repository, and read the score beside the underlying commit acceleration, push recency, contributor activity, and concentration. The browser calls GitHub directly, so the result is reproducible and no GitDealFlow account is required.

Then ask whether that evidence answers your sourcing question. A sharp increase in public commits can justify a closer look at a technical company. A quiet result can mean the work moved to private repositories, the company is not software-led, or there simply is no public engineering signal. It does not prove that a company is weak. This is why GitDealFlow works as an early filter rather than a complete company database.

A simple workflow for using both kinds of signal

  1. Use broad launch and community momentum to notice products gaining public attention.
  2. Use the GitHub checker when the company has meaningful public software activity.
  3. Put companies with a clear change in engineering pace onto a weekly watchlist.
  4. Verify funding history, market, revenue, team, and customer evidence in your normal diligence stack.

This sequence keeps the tools in their proper roles. Attention tells you what people are discussing. Public engineering activity tells you whether a visible technical team has changed pace. Neither replaces founder conversations or diligence, but together they can narrow a large discovery set into a smaller list worth investigating.
