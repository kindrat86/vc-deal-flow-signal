A beforeVC alternative for investors who source technical startups

GitDealFlow is not a broad startup-buzz tracker. It reads public GitHub engineering acceleration to help angels, scouts, and seed investors find technical teams worth investigating before the funding round is public.

Choose GitDealFlow when your question is:
Which technical startups are visibly building fast enough that I should meet the founder before the round is public?

How GitDealFlow differs from beforeVC

beforeVC is useful for broad startup momentum across GitHub, Hacker News, Reddit, and Product Hunt. GitDealFlow takes a narrower approach. It watches public commit velocity, contributor growth, and repository expansion, then explains the engineering pattern in plain English.

That makes GitDealFlow a better fit when you invest in AI, infrastructure, developer tools, enterprise SaaS, or other technical companies where public product work is a useful sourcing clue. It is not the better choice for every company: stealth teams, closed-source products, and non-technical businesses may not show enough public GitHub activity to evaluate.

Use GitDealFlow to discover, then verify

GitDealFlow is the early-warning layer. Use it to find technical companies early. Use Crunchbase, Dealroom, or PitchBook to verify funding history, investors, and market context once a company is on your list. The tools have different jobs, and using them in that order is more useful than treating any one of them as a complete sourcing stack.

What is documented: GitDealFlow refreshes public GitHub signals weekly across 350+ startup orgs in 15 sectors. In a documented panel of 219 startup-period observations across 55 startups, the signal appeared 21 to 47 days before the public fundraise announcement. It is a sourcing signal, not investment advice.

Pick beforeVC if this is your priority

Pick beforeVC if you want broader public attention and breakout-product discovery, and you care more about launch, social, community, and cross-platform momentum than a technical engineering signal tied to a specific sourcing workflow.

What a GitDealFlow sourcing run looks like

Start with the weekly short list, not a blank search box. Read the plain-English reason attached to each company, then open the public evidence for the names that match your thesis. Check whether the change is sustained, whether more contributors are joining, and whether the repository activity reflects product work rather than a one-off maintenance burst. The output is a queue for human review. It does not replace a founder conversation, market work, customer references, or financial diligence.

Where this signal is weak

GitDealFlow is less useful when a company builds entirely in private, has no meaningful public repository, or works in a category where software output is not central to the business. It can also miss a strong company whose public engineering work stays quiet by design. That boundary matters: a visible acceleration signal tells you where to look sooner, not which company to fund. If your thesis depends more on product launches, community discussion, or social attention, beforeVC may give you the broader view you need.

Compare the tools on the same weekly question

Run both products against one sector for several weeks. Record which names each one surfaces, what evidence is attached, and whether the output gives you a clear next research step. Compare the quality of the short list, not the number of alerts. The better tool is the one that repeatedly puts relevant companies in front of you early enough to act, with evidence you can verify.
