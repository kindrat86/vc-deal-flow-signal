# Brunson Workbook 4: Creating Belief (Speed Run)
## vc-deal-flow-signal | 2026-04-13

## Epiphany Bridge Story (use in email 1 long-form)
I was tracking a small fintech startup, mostly out of curiosity. Nothing special on the surface: no press, no AngelList buzz, no warm intros circulating. But their GitHub told a different story. In two weeks, their commit velocity tripled. Four new contributors joined. They spun up three new infrastructure repos. I flagged it in my notes. Three weeks later, they announced a $4M Series A led by a top-tier fund. The investors who got in had seen something I missed, or maybe they just knew someone. That's when it clicked: the signal was right there in the commit graph the whole time. Public, free, updating in real time. Nobody was reading it. I decided to build something that would.

## False Beliefs to Break (use as subject lines for emails 2 to 5)
- Belief 1 (Vehicle): "GitHub data is too noisy to be a real signal"
- Belief 2 (Internal): "I already have enough deal flow from my network"
- Belief 3 (External): "Public data can't give me an edge, everyone has access to it"

## Vehicle / Internal / External Beliefs (use as email body content)

### Vehicle Belief: "GitHub data is too noisy to be a real signal"
**Break it:** Raw GitHub data is noisy, yes. But so is raw stock market data before you apply a screener. We don't show you commit counts. We show you acceleration patterns: when a company's engineering velocity deviates sharply from its own baseline. That's not noise, that's a regime change. In our analysis, companies showing a 2x contributor spike within 14 days have repeatedly preceded fundraise announcements or major product launches within 60 days. We're still building the dataset to quantify this precisely, but the pattern is consistent enough to be actionable.

### Internal Belief: "I already have enough deal flow from my network"
**Break it:** Your network shows you what other investors are already seeing. That's table stakes, not an edge. The deals that generate outsized returns are the ones where you arrive before consensus forms. When five investors are looking at the same deck, terms get worse and you lose leverage. VC Deal Flow Signal surfaces companies that haven't entered the pitch circuit yet, because their engineering is accelerating but their fundraise hasn't started. That's the window your network can't give you.

### External Belief: "Public data can't give me an edge, everyone has access to it"
**Break it:** Everyone has access to SEC filings too. Quant funds still make billions parsing them faster and smarter. The edge isn't in having exclusive data, it's in reading what others ignore. Right now, zero investor tools package GitHub activity as a deal flow signal. The data is public. The analysis layer doesn't exist. That gap is your edge, and it stays your edge until the market catches up.
